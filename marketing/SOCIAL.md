# Mitothrum — social playbook

How we post on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, Threads and Pinterest, and
how social ties into the site. RULES.md (especially sections 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8)
still wins on any conflict. Adopted Oct 26, 2026 from a Muse research playbook
after review: what was kept, changed or rejected is recorded at the bottom.

## Ground rules for every post
- Wellness and cosmetic language only (RULES 4.1): routines, habits, how a
  device is used, what a study found. No disease, treatment, cure, detox or
  guaranteed-outcome claims; no before/after of a person or result.
- **Disclosure at the point of promotion** (RULES 8.1–8.3), on every post that
  mentions a product or links to the site's product pages:
  1. first line of the caption / top of the description: "I earn commissions
     on purchases made through links in this post." Add #ad.
  2. on screen for the whole video, and spoken when there's a voiceover, in the
     video's language;
  3. in the pin description itself, not only on the destination page;
  4. platform labels too: YouTube "includes paid promotion," TikTok commercial
     content disclosure, Meta's labels where offered — in addition to ours.
- **No "I use this" unless it's true** (RULES 2.3). A routine video shows real
  personal use only; otherwise talk about what the research and manuals say.
  Never "we tested it," never invented or quoted reviews, never prices
  (RULES 2.1–2.2; use $/$$/$$$).
- **Images:** original lifestyle or concept imagery (AI-generated is allowed
  for scenes and concepts), but never an AI-generated or copied depiction of a
  real product (RULES 6.1). Turn on the platform's AI label (YouTube altered
  or synthetic content, TikTok AI-generated label, Meta AI info) whenever
  realistic AI imagery or video is used.
- Music and footage licensed or our own (RULES 6.2). Never buy followers,
  views or comments (RULES 8.5).

## Where posts send people
- To the matching comparison or hub page on mitothrum.com, not straight to a
  retailer. The page carries the full disclosure, who should skip a product and
  the Amazon/brand links. No bare Amazon links in captions.
- Any social account that will ever carry an Amazon link must be listed in
  Associates Central first (check the current Amazon policy before doing it).
- UTM-tag every link: `?utm_source=<platform>&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<page>`
  (platform = youtube, instagram, tiktok, facebook, threads, pinterest). GA4
  then shows which platform drives affiliate_click events.

## Content pillars
1. **Educate (most posts):** "What the Science Actually Says" — one finding per
   piece, with the study named; who should skip a device; how to use it safely.
   Captions reuse wording already checked on the page.
2. **Routine:** how a ritual fits into a morning or wind-down, shown as a
   process. Real personal use only (see above).
3. **Connect:** follower questions, polls ("LED mask or microcurrent first?"),
   answers that point to the comparison page; no medical advice.
4. **Promote (no more than 1 in 5 posts):** "Full comparison on the site" +
   link + disclosure.

## One master, many assets (the core workflow)
For each comparison page:
1. One long-form YouTube video, or a script if there's no time.
2. A clean 1080×1920 master (no watermarks), uploaded natively to YouTube
   Shorts, TikTok and Instagram Reels, each with its own caption, cover and
   hook. Burned-in captions in the upper-centre safe zone.
3. Shorts link to the long-form with YouTube's Related Video; long-form gets
   chapters (first at 0:00), a pinned question, an end screen with one video +
   one playlist, and goes into a topic playlist.
4. Two or three 2:3 pins (1000×1500) per page with keyword-rich descriptions
   and the disclosure.
5. A carousel for Instagram/Facebook and a short text version for Threads.

YouTube habits worth keeping: specific hook in the first 1–3 seconds; front-load
titles; reply to comments in the first hours; use Test & Compare on titles and
thumbnails once available; check retention drop-offs before the next video.

## Cadence (a floor, not a growth promise)
Batch once a week, driven by the one-comparison-per-week priority (RULES 7):
- 1 long-form every 1–2 weeks (or skip and make Shorts only)
- 2–3 Shorts/Reels/TikToks per week, all from the same master clips
- Pinterest 3–5 pins/week
- Facebook and Threads 1–2/week each, reusing the same pieces

Review monthly: GA4 sessions and affiliate_click by utm_source — not followers.

## Reviewed and not adopted (from the Muse playbook)
- "Show your routine: I use X like this" as a default format: only when it's
  literally true; otherwise it's an invented endorsement (RULES 2.3).
- "Behind the scenes of testing a routine": implies product testing that
  hasn't happened.
- Instagram 3–5 posts/week plus daily Stories and TikTok up to 5/week on top
  of YouTube: beyond the owner's time budget; the floors above replace it.
- Pins or Shorts with direct retailer links as the main path: the site page is
  the bridge, so the disclosure and context travel with the click.
- Growth multipliers ("2–3×", "10× revenue from a bridge page"): unverified;
  never quoted.
